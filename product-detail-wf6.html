About This Gas

Tungsten
Hexa-
fluoride

Tungsten hexafluoride is the dominant precursor gas for chemical vapor deposition of metallic tungsten films in semiconductor manufacturing. With a boiling point of just 17.1 °C, WF₆ exists as a liquid slightly below room temperature and is stored and shipped as a liquefied gas under its own vapor pressure. This near-ambient vapor pressure makes it exceptionally convenient for delivery into CVD reactors at controlled, reproducible flow rates — a critical advantage for uniformity-sensitive deposition processes.

In W-CVD, WF₆ is reduced by silane (SiH₄) or hydrogen (H₂) at 300–450 °C to deposit conformal tungsten metal. The silane-reduction nucleation step forms thin tungsten nucleation layers on titanium nitride barriers, followed by bulk fill using the hydrogen-reduction reaction. This process fills submicron contact holes and vias with near-void-free tungsten plugs, enabling the vertical interconnect structures that connect transistors across multiple metal layers in advanced DRAM, NAND flash, and leading-edge logic technologies.

Purity is paramount: metallic impurities such as molybdenum (from precursor synthesis), oxygen-bearing species (WO₂F₂, WOF₄), and moisture must be controlled at or below single-digit ppb levels to prevent junction leakage, contact resistance drift, and particle-induced yield loss. Airocean Gases offers WF₆ at three electronic grades — 5N, 5.5N, and 6N — with full lot traceability, sub-ppb metallic impurity analysis by ICP-MS, and dedicated stainless-steel fill equipment to maintain gas integrity from cylinder to tool.
